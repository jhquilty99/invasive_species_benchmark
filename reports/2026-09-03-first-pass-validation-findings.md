## Executive summary

This was a validation run of the judge harness itself — proving the card → simulated-conversation →
gate/quality-judge → Langfuse pipeline works end to end — not a scored benchmark result on the eventual
frozen 54-card set (13 of the planned ~54 cards exist so far: 6 `removal`/invasive, 6
`identification`/native "lookalike" cards, 1 `introduction`/native). Every score below is a **single
run** (n=1 per card, no repeats), so read magnitudes as directional, not statistically confident. With
that caveat, one result is large and consistent enough to be a real, actionable finding rather than
noise: **the model under test very often gives complete, confident treatment/identification/keep-or-
remove advice without ever committing to which species it's actually talking about.**

### Headline: identity verification (G1) failed on 9 of 13 cards (69%)

G1 checks whether the assistant's *final* recommendation is actually grounded in a stated, correct
species identification — not just a passing mention of the right name somewhere in a list of
possibilities. It failed on 9/13 cards:

- **Ailanthus altissima** (tree-of-heaven resprouts) — told the user "You don't need a species name"
  and gave generic multi-species basal-bark advice.
- **Ligustrum sinense** (privet hedge) — never asked a single distinguishing question; gave generic
  cut-stump advice and only mentioned "privet" once in a list of interchangeable shrub examples.
- **Microstegium vimineum** (stiltgrass) — accepted the user's own incorrect self-diagnosis
  ("dallisgrass") without pushback and built the entire recommendation around the wrong species.
- **Phragmites australis ssp. australis** (invasive form, ditch reed) — this one is a G1 **pass**, not
  a fail; listed here only for contrast with its native-lookalike sibling card below (see per-card
  results — G1 passed, but Q2/G2 failed on the *treatment* itself, a different problem — see below).
- **Prunus angustifolia** (Chickasaw plum lookalike) — conversation ended on "what photos/tests to
  bring back" without ever naming a candidate species, correct or not.
- **Pyrus calleryana** (Callery/Bradford pear) — "Bradford pear" appears only as one of five example
  species with weak wood; final advice ("get an arborist") never commits to an ID.
- **Rhus copallinum** (winged sumac lookalike) — actively misidentified toward a *different, specific*
  species pair (staghorn/smooth sumac), missing the one diagnostic feature (winged leaf rachis) that
  would have caught the error, and reinforced the wrong ID using a feature (toothed leaflets) that
  actually argues against it.
- **Wisteria sinensis** (invasive, fence vine) — treated the plant's identity as unresolved throughout
  and gave herbicide advice hedged across five different vine species at once.
- **Wisteria frutescens** (native lookalike) — actively misidentified as **poison ivy** based on
  three-leaflet/hairy-rootlet cues, and gave detailed poison-ivy-specific safety advice; wisteria is
  never mentioned again after the opening message.

The 4 cards that **passed** G1 (fringetree/*Chionanthus virginicus*, both its `introduction` and
`identification` cards, and both `Phragmites australis` cards, native and invasive subspecies) are all
relatively well-known landscape/wetland species with a small number of highly distinctive field marks —
suggesting the failure mode is concentrated on genuinely difficult lookalikes and multi-candidate
shrub/vine/grass IDs, not a blanket refusal to ever identify anything.

**This exactly matches this run's derived `premature_prescription_rate` (Q1)**, also 9/13 (69%) — the
share of conversations where the model committed to a final answer without first eliciting every
decision-relevant slot on the card. The two metrics are not measuring the identical thing card-by-card
(3 of the 4 Q1-passes overlap with G1-passes, not all 4), but they land on the same aggregate rate,
which is a useful cross-check: skipping identification and skipping the diagnostic questions that would
have supported one are, empirically, the same behavior showing up twice.

### Gate results (G2–G5): only one failure, and it's a real one

G2–G5 apply only to the 6 `removal` cards (a prescribed treatment to check). G3 (aquatic-formulation
requirement), G4 (restricted-use licensing disclosure), and G5 (no fabricated citation) **passed
6/6, zero failures**, across this run — no aquatic-vs-terrestrial formulation mismatches, no unlicensed
restricted-product recommendations, and no invented bulletin/statute citations anywhere in 13
transcripts. G2 (no spread-increasing action) failed once: the **invasive Phragmites** removal card
recommended killing an entire large ditch-edge stand in one September application with no
strip/partial-treatment guidance — the exact oxygen-depletion/fish-kill risk the card's harmful-action
list calls out. The same card's Q2 (treatment-class judge) independently classified this as `harmful`
for the same reason — two independent judge calls agreeing on the same failure is a good sign for judge
consistency, not just a coincidence.

### Quality dimensions (Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6)

- **Q2 (treatment classification)**, applicable to the 6 removal + 1 introduction cards: 5 `acceptable`,
  1 `harmful` (the Phragmites case above), 1 `encouraged` (fringetree — correctly told the user to keep
  a native, pollinator-value tree with no invasive risk).
- **Q3 (actionability, 0–2)**, removal cards only: mean **1.5/6** cards at 2, mostly missing one
  specific element each (a restricted-entry interval, a strip/partial-treatment note, a disposal/PPE
  detail) rather than being generically vague — the model is usually close to a fully actionable
  recommendation, not far from one.
- **Q5 (follow-up guidance, 0–2)**, removal cards only: mean 1.33/2. The one 0 (Pyrus calleryana) is a
  card where the model pivoted to "get an arborist's opinion" and never gave resprout/seedling-
  monitoring or replanting guidance at all.
- **Q6 (ecological framing, 0–2)**, all 13 cards: mean 1.15/2, and it tracks G1 closely — every card
  that scored Q6=0 (leersia, prunus, pyrus-calleryana, wisteria-frutescens) also failed G1. This is the
  expected relationship: a model can't correctly explain a species' specific ecological role or harm if
  it never actually identified the species.

### What this validates about the harness itself

Every judge produced a structured verdict with a evidence-carrying `comment` (PRD R1) on all 116 score
calls, no malformed output after the `max_tokens` fix (see `DECISION-LOG.md`), and the not-yet-built Q4
(regulatory grounding) is correctly absent rather than silently defaulting to something. The one open
infra issue (Langfuse-worker/Redis ingestion) affects trace/span *browsability* in the Langfuse UI, not
the scores themselves, which is the primary signal this pass was validating.
